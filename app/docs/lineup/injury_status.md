For the NFL, injury markings on depth charts generally fall into two categories: **game-status designations** (used for the weekly injury report) and **roster designations** (used for players who are unavailable for multiple weeks).

### 🏈 NFL Game-Status Designations

These are the abbreviations you will see next to a player's name on a depth chart for a specific week's game. They reflect the official injury report categories.

| Abbreviation | Full Name | Definition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **O** | **Out** | The player is definitively not playing in the upcoming game. He is ineligible to play and should not be started in fantasy lineups. |
| **D** | **Doubtful** | The player is unlikely to play and has roughly a 25% chance of being active. Teams rarely use this designation, and it generally means you should prepare for him to be unavailable. |
| **Q** | **Questionable** | The player's status is uncertain, representing roughly a 50-50 chance he will play. This is the most common designation and is often a true game-time decision that requires monitoring up until kickoff. |
| **P** | **Probable** | The player is likely to play and is expected to be available. In recent years, the NFL has moved away from using this designation frequently, but some platforms and older charts still include it. |

### 📋 NFL Roster Designations

These abbreviations indicate a player is on a reserve list and will miss a significant amount of time (typically a minimum of four games). They do not count against the team's active 53-man roster.

| Abbreviation | Full Name | Definition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **IR** | **Injured Reserve** | A player with a football-related injury who is placed on this list and must miss at least four games. Teams can designate a limited number of players to return from IR during the season. Players on IR do not count against the 53-man roster but their salaries still count against the cap. |
| **PUP** | **Physically Unable to Perform** | Used for players with a football-related injury that occurred before or during training camp. If a player is still on the Active/PUP list at final roster cuts, he can be moved to Reserve/PUP and must miss at least the first four games of the regular season. He does not count against the 53-man roster but is paid his full salary. |
| **NFI** | **Non-Football Injury** | For players who suffered an injury or illness **outside of NFL football activities** (e.g., an offseason accident, illness, or injury from college). Like PUP, if placed on Reserve/NFI at final cuts, the player must miss at least four games and does not count against the active roster. A key difference from PUP is that players on NFI are **not entitled to their base salary**. |
